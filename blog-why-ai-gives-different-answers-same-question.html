Why AI Gives Different Answers to the Same Question (And How to Fix It)

Published October 19, 2026 · 8 min read · AI Prompting
You asked it twice. You got two different answers. If that sounds familiar, you're not alone — and it's not your imagination. A Washington State University study found that ChatGPT gives consistent answers only 73% of the time when asked the same question ten times. Across the AI community, the complaint is constant: "I asked it the same thing and got something completely different." Here's exactly why it happens and how to fix it.

Why Does AI Give Different Answers to the Same Question?

When people ask this question online, the answers are almost always some version of "that's just how AI works." That's technically true — but it's incomplete. Understanding the actual mechanism gives you real power to control it.

Large language models like ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini don't "look up" answers like a search engine. They generate text word by word, at each step picking the next word from a probability distribution over every possible word in their vocabulary. That process is inherently probabilistic. Even with an identical prompt in a fresh conversation, the model is running the same probabilistic selection — and small differences in which words it picks first cascade into meaningfully different answers.

Four main factors drive this variability:

  • Temperature setting — This directly controls how much randomness the model allows in word selection. Higher temperature = more surprising outputs. Lower temperature = more predictable ones.
  • Top_p (nucleus sampling) — This filters which words the model even considers. A high top_p includes more marginal options; a low top_p restricts choices to the most likely words only.
  • Conversation context — Any prior messages in the conversation subtly shift the probability distributions the model uses. A question asked after a long discussion lands differently than the same question in a fresh chat.
  • Model batching and hardware — Minor non-determinism can enter through how the model's computations are batched across GPU hardware, and in floating-point arithmetic on different runs.

The Two Settings That Control AI Consistency

Most users never touch these settings — and that's exactly why they keep getting surprised by AI's variability. Two knobs in every AI platform control the randomness of your outputs:

Temperature

Temperature is a scaling factor on the probability distribution the model uses to pick each word. Think of it as a creativity dial:

  • Temperature 0 — Almost purely deterministic. The model almost always picks the single most probable next word. Best for: factual answers, code, structured output, anything you need to replicate exactly.
  • Temperature 0.3–0.5 — Balanced. Some randomness creeps in but the output stays focused. Good for: general writing, analysis, explanations.
  • Temperature 0.7–1.0+ — High creativity. The model explores less probable word choices, producing surprising, diverse, and occasionally wild outputs. Best for: brainstorming, creative writing, idea generation.

Most AI platforms default to a mid-range temperature (typically 0.7–1.0), which is tuned for chat friendliness — friendly chat likes variety — but is a poor fit for anyone who needs consistent, repeatable results.

Top_p (Nucleus Sampling)

Top_p controls which words the model considers at each step. It represents the cumulative probability threshold: setting top_p to 0.9 means the model only considers the smallest set of words that together account for 90% of the probability mass, and ignores the long tail of less-likely words.

The interaction between temperature and top_p matters. A common guideline: adjust one or the other, not both at once. For consistent outputs, set temperature to 0.1 or below and leave top_p at its default — or set top_p to 0.7 for a tighter word selection pool.

❌ Default Settings

Temperature: ~0.7–1.0
Top_p: ~0.9–1.0

Outputs vary significantly between runs. Good for exploration, bad for reproducibility. You may get a different answer every time.

✅ For Consistent Results

Temperature: 0.1 or below
Top_p: 0.7–0.9

Most likely word is chosen almost every time. Results are stable across runs. Better for task completion, coding, and factual work.

How to Get Consistent AI Results Every Time

Beyond settings, your prompt structure is the single biggest lever for consistent outputs. The vaguer your prompt, the more the model has to fill in the gaps with its statistical average — which is exactly what produces the generic, vanilla answers users complain about.

1. Lock Your Output Format Explicitly

One of the most reliable ways to get consistent results is to tell the model exactly what format you want — and what to avoid. Compare these two prompts:

Generic prompt → Variable output "Give me advice on improving my code." Structured prompt → Consistent output "Give me exactly 3 specific suggestions to improve this code. For each suggestion: (1) the problem, (2) the fix, (3) why it matters. Format as a numbered list. Do not add any preamble or summary."

The second prompt narrows the solution space dramatically. The model has fewer degrees of freedom, so each run produces nearly identical output.

2. Use Constraints, Not Adjectives

Phrases like "be professional," "keep it concise," "make it interesting" are interpreted against a statistical average of what millions of people consider "professional" or "concise." The result is generically professional — correct, but forgettable. Instead, use concrete constraints:

  • Instead of "write professionally" → "use active voice, short sentences (under 15 words), and no jargon"
  • Instead of "keep it brief" → "maximum 3 sentences, each conveying one concrete point"
  • Instead of "be creative" → "start with a question, end with a specific action the reader can take today"

3. Add Examples (Few-Shot Prompting)

If you need highly consistent output for a specific task, show the model an example of exactly what you want. This is called few-shot prompting — providing 2–3 examples of the input/output pattern you expect:

Few-shot prompt structure "Here are three examples of good code review feedback: 1. [input: long function] → [output: 2-sentence critique, one specific refactor suggestion] 2. [input: unclear variable name] → [output: 2-sentence critique, clear renamed alternative] 3. [input: missing error handling] → [output: 2-sentence critique, minimal code fix] Now review this code: [your code here]"

Few-shot prompting dramatically reduces variability because the model isn't inferring what you want — you're showing it directly.

4. Separate the Thinking from the Output

Chain-of-thought prompting — asking the model to reason step by step before giving its final answer — doesn't just improve reasoning quality. It also produces more consistent results across runs, because the reasoning trace creates a stable path to the conclusion. Ask the model to "think through this step by step" before answering, and you'll notice both better quality and less variation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does AI give different answers to the same question?

AI models generate text by predicting the most likely next word at each step. Even with the same prompt, this probabilistic process can produce different word choices, sentence structures, and even conclusions. Settings like temperature control how much randomness enters this process, and conversation context from prior messages can shift the model's focus.

Does setting temperature to 0 make AI completely deterministic?

Temperature 0 removes most but not all randomness. Minor non-determinism can still come from how the model batches computation, variations in floating-point arithmetic, and underlying hardware differences. For most practical purposes, temperature 0.1 or below gives highly consistent results for factual and task-oriented queries.

How do I stop AI from giving different answers every time?

Use temperature 0.1 or below for factual, consistent outputs. Structure your prompts with explicit constraints: output format, audience, tone, and what to avoid. Adding concrete examples of the output you want (few-shot prompting) dramatically narrows the variation in AI responses.

What is top_p in AI settings?

Top_p (nucleus sampling) controls which words the AI considers. A top_p of 0.9 means the model only considers the smallest set of words whose combined probability adds up to 90%. Lower top_p values restrict choices to the most probable words, reducing surprise and increasing consistency.

Why does the same prompt work differently on ChatGPT vs Claude vs Gemini?

Each AI model has different training data, architecture, and default settings. What works as a prompt on one AI may need restructuring for another.
