Why Google Business Profile is harder to schedule than Facebook

Every other network hands you a straightforward API call: create a post, attach media, set a publish time, done. The Google My Business API does the same thing, but it is wrapped in a gate that catches almost everyone on the first attempt.

The API is not self-serve. Before the endpoint appears in your Google Cloud Console, you have to submit an access request through Google's own approval form. Approval typically takes a few days and can stretch to a full week. Until then, the Google My Business API is simply invisible in the API library, and any tutorial that skips this step will leave you stuck at a 403 with no explanation.

There is a second wrinkle: the integration needs three separate Google APIs enabled, not one. Enable two and you get a permission error that looks like a broken app.

Required Google Cloud configuration # 1. Create or select a Google Cloud project # 2. Enable all THREE APIs (the first only appears after approval): # - Google My Business API # - My Business Account Management API # - My Business Business Information API # 3. OAuth redirect URIs on your Web application credential: https://your-postiz-domain.com/integrations/social/gmb http://localhost:4200/integrations/social/gmb # local dev http://localhost:5000/integrations/social/gmb # Docker # 4. .env credentials (same pair you may already use for YouTube) GOOGLE_GMB_CLIENT_ID="your-client-id" GOOGLE_GMB_CLIENT_SECRET="your-client-secret"

That redirect URI list is straight from the official Postiz Google My Business provider documentation, and it is worth copying exactly. Miss the trailing path and the OAuth handshake fails in a way that produces a blank error page rather than a useful message.

What Google's own free scheduler does and does not do

Google added native post scheduling to the Business Profile dashboard in late 2025, and by 2026 it can publish the same update across multiple locations in one action. That is genuinely good news for a single-location business, and it is free.

What it is not is a marketing system. The native screen schedules posts and stops there.

  • No approval workflow. A franchise manager cannot route an update to a reviewer before it goes live.
  • No cross-posting. One announcement still means separately posting to Facebook, Instagram and LinkedIn.
  • No content library. You write every post from scratch, every time.
  • No calendar spanning other networks. Your Google posts sit apart from everything else you publish.
  • No AI drafting tuned to Google's 1,500-character format.

Two errors worth recognising immediately

If you cannot find the Google My Business API in the library, your access request is still pending — check your email or resubmit. If you get a 403, one of the three APIs is missing, and the fix is in the Cloud Console rather than in Postiz. If your analytics chart is empty, that is a separate issue: the performance API refuses certain metric and date-range combinations, and it returns nothing at all instead of throwing an error for newer or unverified profiles.

What to actually publish on a Business Profile

Scheduling is only half the job. Google Posts fall into three practical types, and each has a different job to do.

  • What's New posts — product updates, new services, seasonal changes. Your workhorse cadence, usually weekly.
  • Offer posts — a discount or promotion with an expiry date. These are the ones that drive calls and direction requests, and they are worth scheduling tightly around the promotion window.
  • Event posts — classes, openings, webinars. Schedule them as soon as they're confirmed, since the date is baked into the post.

Two formatting rules matter more than anything else. Keep the opening 150 characters carrying the offer or the news, because Google promotes that text to a headline above the post body. Stay under 1,500 characters.
