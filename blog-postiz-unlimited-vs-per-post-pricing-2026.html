The Pricing Problem with Mainstream Schedulers

Buffer and Hootsuite built their pricing models when social media marketing was simpler. You had a Facebook page, maybe a Twitter account, and you paid a flat fee to manage them. In 2026, that math falls apart fast.

Modern creators and agencies juggle 10 to 20 social channels across platforms that did not exist five years ago. TikTok, Reddit communities, Telegram channels — each one is a legitimate publishing target. Mainstream schedulers charge for each one.

Buffer's Per-Post Math

Buffer's classic pain point is the "10 posts per channel" limit on its $6/month Starter plan. Post three times a day on Instagram and you've burned through your allowance before the week is over. The $12/month per-user plan removes the post cap but adds a per-seat cost that scales fast for teams.

Hootsuite's Per-Channel Trap

Hootsuite Organizer starts at $99/month and limits you to 10 social accounts. For agencies managing multiple client brands, the Professional plan at $299/month with 35 social accounts fills up quickly. Enterprise pricing — reportedly $999+/month — is out of reach for most solo creators and small agencies.

Postiz Pricing: The Self-Hosted Free Tier Changes the Math

Postiz takes a fundamentally different approach. Its cloud service offers tiered pricing, but the open-source version runs on your own server via Docker. That means unlimited posts, unlimited channels, and zero recurring fees — limited only by the API rate limits of the platforms you connect.

The catch: you need a server. A $6/month VPS from providers like DigitalOcean, Hetzner, or Linode is enough to run Postiz for most single-user or small-team setups. Add a domain and SSL certificate (Let's Encrypt is free), and your total infrastructure cost is roughly $6–$10 per month versus $12–$299 per month for equivalent access on Buffer or Hootsuite.
