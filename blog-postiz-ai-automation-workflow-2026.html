Step 1: Connect Your Social Accounts

After signing up for Postiz, the first automation step is connecting the platforms you post to. Postiz uses OAuth for most networks — authorize each account once and it stays connected.

For Instagram, TikTok, and Facebook business accounts, you'll need to connect through Meta's official API. YouTube requires a connected Google account. LinkedIn, Reddit, and Pinterest each have their own authorization flow handled directly inside Postiz.

Pro tip: Group accounts by content type

Instead of listing every account flat, create folders inside Postiz for different content streams — one for short-form video content, one for blog repurposes, one for thought-leadership posts. This keeps your queue organized and makes bulk automation much easier to audit.

Step 2: Set Up Your AI Content Agent

Postiz's AI agent is the engine that makes hands-off posting realistic. Here's how to configure it:

  1. Define your content brief — set the tone (professional, casual, witty), target audience, and topics you regularly post about. The AI uses this to generate on-brand copy.
  2. Choose content inputs — paste a blog URL, upload an image, or type a short prompt. The agent produces platform-specific captions, hashtags, and suggested posting times.
  3. Preview or auto-publish — you can review each AI-generated post before it enters the queue, or enable auto-publish for a true set-it-and-forget-it workflow.

The AI agent also learns from your posting history. If you consistently edit its outputs in a certain direction (shorter captions, more hashtags, different tone), it adjusts future generations accordingly.

Step 3: Build Your Queue-Based Posting Schedule

The queue is where Postiz automation gets serious. Instead of scheduling individual posts one by one, you fill a queue and let Postiz drain it on your configured schedule.

Schedule vs. Queue — what's the difference?

  • Schedule: you pick a specific date and time for each post. Good for campaign launches and product announcements.
  • Queue: posts sit in a waiting list and go live at your set posting frequency. Perfect for evergreen content, tips, and filler posts that don't have a specific launch window.

Most automation workflows use both: a content calendar for planned campaigns, plus an evergreen queue that continuously publishes without any further input.

Optimal posting frequency by platform (2026 data)

  • TikTok: 1–3 posts per day
  • Instagram: 1–2 posts per day + 3–5 Stories
  • X/Twitter: 3–10 tweets per day
  • LinkedIn: 1 post per day
  • Facebook: 1–2 posts per day
  • YouTube: 2–3 uploads per week
  • Reddit: 1–3 posts per day (subreddit-dependent)

Step 4: Automate Cross-Platform Repurposing

One of Postiz's most powerful automation features is content repurposing across platforms automatically. A single YouTube video, for example, can become:

  • A TikTok clip with a short hook and link in bio
  • An Instagram Reel with the same video trimmed to 60 seconds
  • A LinkedIn carousel summarizing the key insight
  • A Twitter/X thread breaking down the main takeaways
  • A Reddit post in a relevant subreddit with a discussion prompt

Postiz handles this by letting you create a "parent post" and then configure which platforms receive adapted versions. You write once; the AI helps adapt it for each platform's format and audience expectations.

Step 5: Integrate with n8n, Zapier, or Make.com

Postiz's REST API opens up advanced automation possibilities. If you run content creation in other tools, you can pipe outputs directly into Postiz's queue without opening the dashboard.

Example: n8n workflow trigger // When a new blog post is published on your site, // n8n calls the Postiz API to create a scheduled post POST https://api.postiz.com/v1/posts { "content": "New blog post: {{ $json.title }}", "platforms": ["twitter", "linkedin", "facebook"], "scheduledAt": "{{ $json.publishedDate }}", "media": ["{{ $json.featuredImage }}"] }

The Postiz API supports creating posts, uploading media, managing calendars, and fetching analytics — everything you need to build a fully automated content pipeline that starts with a single trigger (new blog publish, new product drop, new customer review).

Step 6: Monitor Performance and Let Data Drive the Queue

Postiz includes built-in analytics that show you which posts, platforms, and posting times generate the most engagement. The automation loop closes when you use these insights to tune your queue schedule.

  • Best posting times: Postiz surfaces your audience's peak activity windows — set these as your queue drain times.
  • Top-performing content types: if video clips outperform static posts, route more video through your queue.
  • Platform-specific performance: if LinkedIn drives far more leads than Twitter, increase LinkedIn frequency and decrease Twitter's queue allocation.
