How to Write Prompts That Work Across Different AI Chatbots

Published October 8, 2026 · 9 min read

You wrote a great prompt. It worked perfectly on ChatGPT. Then you pasted it into Claude and got a completely different response. Run it through Gemini and it falls apart entirely. The prompt has not changed, but the chatbot has. This guide explains why that happens and gives you a transferable framework for writing prompts that deliver consistent results across any AI model.

Why AI Prompts Behave Differently Across Chatbots

Every major AI chatbot — ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok, DeepSeek, and others — is built on a different language model with its own training history, reinforcement learning signals, and personality tuning. That divergence is the root cause of cross-chatbot prompt failure.

Here are the specific ways models differ:

  • Inference style: ChatGPT tends to infer user intent from partial instructions. Claude waits for explicit direction and treats ambiguity as a reason to ask clarifying questions rather than guess. Gemini leans toward factual precision and may default to longer, more structured answers.
  • System prompt handling: Claude processes system-level instructions with high fidelity. ChatGPT may re-interpret them based on conversation context. Gemini in some interfaces ignores or deprioritizes system prompts in favor of user-level instructions.
  • Output length defaults: Claude capped responses at roughly 4,096 tokens in many configurations. ChatGPT with GPT-4o defaults to a higher fluid limit. Gemini may truncate or stop early on tasks it considers complete before you expect.
  • Safety and refusal thresholds: Claude refuses ambiguous requests more readily than ChatGPT. Grok has a much lower refusal threshold for topics that other models handle neutrally. A prompt that sits comfortably within one model guardrails may trigger a refusal in another.

These differences do not mean you need to write entirely different prompts for each platform. They mean your prompts need to be more explicit about what you want and how you want it delivered.

The Assumption That Breaks Cross-Chatbot Prompts

Do not assume a model will infer what you mean from incomplete instructions. Inference is where models diverge most. The safer strategy is to spell it out — specificity transfers; ambiguity does not.

The SAFE Framework: Write Once, Use Everywhere

The SAFE framework gives any prompt the structural completeness that makes it model-agnostic. SAFE stands for Situation, Action, Format, and Examples. Apply all four parts and your prompt transfers across ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok, and most other mainstream chatbots without modification.

1. Situation — Set the Context

Open your prompt with one or two sentences that establish the scenario. This replaces guesswork with shared context. The model does not need your life story — just enough ground truth to frame the task correctly.

Example We are a two-person startup with no dedicated copywriter. Our product is project management software for freelance graphic designers.

2. Action — Define the Task

State the specific task in a direct imperative sentence. Avoid vague verbs like "help with" or "do something with." Use precise action verbs: write, analyze, compare, rewrite, list, summarize, classify.

Example Write three email subject lines for a cold outreach campaign targeting this audience.

3. Format — Specify the Output Structure

This is the most transferable part of any prompt and the one most often omitted. Tell the model exactly how to structure its response. Format instructions are followed consistently across nearly all modern models because they are explicit constraints rather than interpretive suggestions.

Example Format each subject line as a single line starting with an emoji followed by no more than 60 characters. Include one curiosity-gap opener and one social-proof variant.

4. Examples — Show What You Want

One or two concrete examples do more than a paragraph of description. Show the input and the exact output you expect. Examples calibrate the model to your standard faster than any amount of stylistic guidance.

Example Example: "🚀 5 design firms saved 12 hours a week with this one tool" — this is the style and structure I want you to match.

Put It Together

A complete SAFE prompt fits in three to five sentences and covers all ambiguity before the model generates a single token. The result is a response that matches your intent on the first try — in ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, or any other chatbot you use.

How to Test a Prompt Across Multiple Chatbots

Testing takes five minutes and tells you everything you need to know about where your prompt transfers and where it breaks down.

  1. Pick one canonical prompt version. Write it using the SAFE framework. Do not optimize for any single model — write for transferability.
  2. Open ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini in separate tabs. Paste the same prompt into all three simultaneously.
  3. Compare outputs on four criteria: accuracy of task completion, output format adherence, response length appropriateness, and factual correctness.
  4. Identify the gap. If one model produces a structurally different answer, add a Format instruction targeting that gap. If a model refuses the task, soften or reframe the sensitive part.
  5. Re-test with the adjusted prompt. The goal is one prompt that scores "good enough" across all three, not a perfect result on one that fails on others.

When Cross-Chatbot Parity Is Not the Goal

If you use Claude specifically for coding and ChatGPT specifically for brainstorming, you do not need identical outputs — you need reliable outputs on each platform. The SAFE framework still helps because it lets you set platform-specific expectations in the Format section without changing the overall structure.

Common Cross-Chatbot Prompt Mistakes

Mistake 1: Relying on Model Inference

Assuming the model will fill in missing context is the single biggest cause of inconsistent results. ChatGPT handles inference well and may cover for a vague prompt in ways Claude and Gemini will not.

Mistake 2: Using Platform-Specific jargon

Some prompting techniques that work well on one platform do not transfer. Chain-of-thought prompting in the style optimized for GPT models may not produce the same reasoning path in Gemini. Keep techniques general and principles-based rather than model-specific.

Mistake 3: Skipping Format Instructions

Format is the most consistently honored instruction across all models. If you only add one thing to a failing prompt, add a sentence describing the exact output structure you want. This single addition fixes more cross-chatbot failures than any other change.

Mistake 4: Using Contractions in Few-Shot Examples

Few-shot examples with contractions like "do not" vs "don't" or "it is" vs "it's" can confuse format-sensitive models in edge cases. Write examples in plain formal English without contractions to maximize cross-model reliability.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my prompt work on ChatGPT but fail on Claude or Gemini?

Different AI models are trained on different datasets with different reinforcement learning signals. ChatGPT is tuned to be helpful in a conversational way, while Claude prioritizes accuracy and safety, and Gemini is optimized for factual recall. A vague prompt that ChatGPT infers well might read as incomplete by Claude, which expects more explicit instructions. The fix is to add specific output format instructions and explicit constraints that reduce ambiguity for all models.

What is the safest prompt format that works on every AI chatbot?

The four-part SAFE format works across all major models: Situation sets the context, Action defines the task, Format specifies the output structure, and Examples provide one or two concrete input-output pairs. Adding explicit Format instructions prevents the model from guessing your preferred structure. Keeping language direct and declarative rather than conversational also reduces the chance of misinterpretation across different model personalities.

Do I need different prompts for ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Grok?

You do not need completely different prompts. The core structure stays the same. Where models diverge is in how much context they need, how they handle system-level instructions, and how they respond to tone cues. A prompt written with the SAFE framework transfers well across models with only minor adjustments to length and specificity. Testing your prompt on two or three models takes five minutes and tells you exactly which adjustments are needed.

How does Prompt Helper Gemini improve prompts for cross-chatbot use?

When you are crafting a prompt you plan to use across multiple chatbots, the extension helps you add the explicit formatting and constraint instructions that make prompts transfer reliably from ChatGPT to Claude to Gemini without losing quality.

Can I use the same few-shot examples across different AI models?

Few-shot examples transfer across models but sometimes need reformatting. Claude and Gemini handle labeled examples in markdown tables very well. ChatGPT responds well to simple numbered lists for examples. Grok prefers plain text without markdown formatting. The principle stays the same: provide one or two representative examples that demonstrate the exact input format and expected output structure you want the model to follow.
