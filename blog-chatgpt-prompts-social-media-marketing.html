You open ChatGPT, type "write a post about our new feature," and get back three sentences that could belong to any brand on earth. The difference between a post that flops and one that gets shared is rarely the model. It is the prompt. This guide gives you 20 copy-paste ChatGPT prompts for social media marketing, organized by job, plus the exact method for upgrading any prompt so the output finally sounds like you.

Why Your Social Media Prompts Sound Generic

ChatGPT is a pattern predictor. When your prompt is vague, it predicts the most statistically average answer — the safest, most forgettable version of what you asked. That is why "write a post about productivity" produces the same content on every feed, while "write a hook-first post for busy founders about why 9-to-5 deep work is dead" produces something with a pulse. Generic output is not a model limit; it is an instruction gap.

The fix is people-first specificity. Google's helpful content guidance rewards content written for real people, and the same principle applies to the prompts that generate it. Marketers feel this pressure daily — Sprout Social reports that 61% of marketers plan to invest more in creator content in 2026, which means audiences are hungrier than ever for voices, not templates. Every prompt below forces the model to behave like a strategist with an opinion, not a bot with a boilerplate.

The Anatomy of a Social Media Prompt That Converts

Every high-performing prompt in this guide follows the same skeleton. Learn it once and you can build your own — or let a prompt enhancer assemble it for you in one click.

  • Role: who the model should be ("you are a social media strategist for a B2B SaaS brand")
  • Context: the business, audience, and product facts it needs
  • Task: exactly what to produce and how many items
  • Constraints: tone, banned words, length, format, and goals
  • Example: one sample of output you actually like

Here is the difference in practice:

Before: "Write a social media post about our new app."

After: "You are a social media strategist for [app], a productivity tool for freelancers. Write 3 Instagram captions for our launch post. Audience: solo freelancers aged 25-40. Each caption: an 80-character hook, two sentences of benefit, and one call to action. No buzzwords. Match the voice of this example: [paste one caption you love]."

The second prompt has guardrails, so the model cannot retreat into average. For more anatomy patterns, see our collection of AI prompt examples and the Prompt Engineering Guide's tips on specificity.

20 ChatGPT Prompts for Social Media Marketing

Copy any prompt, replace the placeholders in brackets, and paste it into ChatGPT. Each one is built to be reusable across a full month of content.

Content Ideas & Strategy Prompts

1. "You are a social media strategist for [business], a [industry] brand serving [audience]. Generate 7 post ideas for [platform] for next week. For each idea include: post format (carousel, reel, image, text, poll), a one-sentence hook, and the goal (awareness, engagement, conversion). Vary the formats across the week."

Why it works: the format and goal fields stop the model from defaulting to "5 generic tips."

2. "Based on these customer pain points, create 5 social posts that each solve one specific problem. Pain points: [list]. For each post, write the hook, the body, and a call to action, and label which pain point it targets. Keep the tone [tone]."

Why it works: real customer language beats invented topics every time.

3. "I run a [business] targeting [audience]. A trending topic in our industry right now is [topic]. Write 3 posts tying this trend to our brand without sounding forced. Use three angles: educational, opinion-based, and discussion-starting. Keep each under [character limit] characters."

Why it works: timeliness plus three distinct angles earns engagement.

4. "Interview me to build a content strategy. Ask me 5 questions one at a time about my business, audience, competitors, and what makes my product different. After I answer, propose 6 content pillars with 3 post ideas per pillar."

Why it works: the model extracts real information instead of inventing strategy.

Caption & Hook Writing Prompts

5. "Write 5 opening hooks for a social post about [topic]. Use these styles: a bold contrarian statement, a surprising statistic, a question, a short story, and a 'stop doing X' warning. Keep each hook under 15 words."

Why it works: hook variety plus a hard length cap produces options you can A/B test.

6. "Here is a draft caption for a [platform] post about [topic]: [paste draft]. Rewrite it as a 3-part caption: an 80-character hook, a 2-3 sentence body that adds value, and a call to action. Match the voice of these examples: [paste 2 of your past posts]."

Why it works: few-shot prompting anchors the model to your real voice.

7. "Turn this product feature into a benefit-driven carousel script: [feature description]. Create 7 slides: title, 5 benefit slides (one benefit, one proof point, one mini-hook each), and a CTA slide. Write the full text for every slide, under 25 words per slide."

Why it works: structured output turns features into scroll-stopping benefit stacks.

8. "Write 3 engagement captions about [topic] that end with a question designed to start a conversation. Each caption must include one personal experience, one opinion, and one practical tip. Max 3 hashtags each."

Why it works: personal details and a hard hashtag cap force authenticity.

Content Calendar & Planning Prompts

9. "Create a 30-day social media content calendar for [business] across [platforms]. Organize by week with themes, and for each day list: post type, topic, goal, and asset needed. Balance the mix at 50% value, 30% engagement, and 20% promotion. Output as a table."

Why it works: the mix ratio and table format make it actionable in one pass.

10. "Build a weekly content plan from this single theme: [theme]. Generate 5 posts that explore different angles of the theme, one per weekday. For each, specify platform, format, hook, and CTA. Do not use generic motivational quotes."

Why it works: the explicit ban on motivational quotes kills the most common filler.

11. "Analyze this posting schedule and recommend a better cadence and format mix: [paste recent post types and dates]. Use 2026 engagement best practices for [platform] and give me a revised weekly schedule as a table with reasons for each change."

Why it works: data-driven planning beats guesswork about when to post.

Platform-Specific Prompts

12. "Write an Instagram carousel about [topic] for [audience]. Slide 1 is a scroll-stopping title, slides 2-5 teach one idea each with an example, slide 6 shares a common mistake, slide 7 is the CTA. Use emoji sparingly, only in the hook or CTA."

Why it works: the 7-slide structure matches what Instagram carousels need to rank.

13. "Write a LinkedIn post about [topic] that positions me as a credible voice. Structure: a bold first line, a 2-3 sentence personal story, one key insight, and a question for the comments. Length: 120-180 words. No buzzwords like 'synergy' or 'game-changer'."

Why it works: banned buzzwords plus a comment-driving question fit LinkedIn's algorithm.

14. "Create a 30-second TikTok or Reels script about [topic]: a 3-second hook, a 15-second demonstration or story, and a 10-second payoff with a CTA. Write it as a shot list with on-screen text suggestions, plus one trending audio idea if relevant."

Why it works: a shot list turns a caption into a producible video plan.

Hashtag & Discovery Prompts

15. "Generate a hashtag strategy for this post: [topic or paste caption]. Give me three sets: 5 high-volume hashtags, 8 mid-tier niche hashtags, and 5 low-competition tags. Explain in one line why each set matters for discovery on [platform]."

Why it works: tiered hashtags reach both broad and targeted audiences.

16. "Use this search data to find content gaps: [paste keyword list or search queries]. Suggest 5 social posts that target questions people are actually asking, with the exact question as the hook."

Why it works: search-driven content wins in social search and AI answers.

17. "Rewrite my [platform] bio and profile description for [business]. Target audience: [audience]. Include what we do, who it is for, and one differentiator in the first 120 characters. Offer 3 versions: playful, professional, and minimal."

Why it works: an optimized bio is free discovery real estate.

Repurposing & Analytics Prompts

18. "Here is a blog post: [paste]. Turn it into 5 social media posts for [platform]: one key takeaway per post, each with a different hook style (question, stat, story, contrarian, how-to). Link back to the blog in the CTA."

Why it works: one asset becomes a week of content with zero new ideas needed.

19. "Here are my last 30 days of post analytics: [paste metrics: reach, engagement, clicks by post type]. Identify 3 patterns, 2 underperforming formats, and 1 new content type to test. Give me a 14-day experiment plan."

Why it works: analytics become a test plan instead of a dashboard you ignore.

20. "Turn this email or newsletter into a social thread for [platform]: [paste]. Keep the core message, add a hook, break it into 4-6 posts, and end with a CTA that drives to [destination]."

Why it works: your best email can do double duty as your best thread.

How to Use These Prompts Like a Pro

Three habits separate marketers who get usable content from those who get drafts:

  • Fill every placeholder. The bracket fields are not decoration. A prompt with your real audience, product, and examples outperforms a generic one every single time.
  • Run it twice and merge. Generate two versions, keep the best hook from each, then paste the winner back for one final polish pass.
  • Add one example of your own. The highest-leverage line in any prompt is "match the voice of this example." One of your best past posts is worth a full paragraph of instructions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the best ChatGPT prompts for social media marketing?

The best prompts combine role, audience, goal, constraints, and format. Strong examples include a week of post ideas by platform, hook-first caption rewrites, a 30-day content calendar, and platform-specific scripts for Instagram, LinkedIn, and TikTok. Copy the templates above, replace the placeholders with your real details, and the output will beat generic prompts every time.

How do I make ChatGPT write in my brand voice?

Give the model examples of your past posts and describe your tone in one sentence. Few-shot prompting works: paste two or three captions you love, then ask ChatGPT to match that voice. Add negative constraints such as no emoji spam or no buzzwords. An AI prompt enhancer can apply this structure automatically when you are in a hurry.

Can ChatGPT create a social media content calendar?

Yes. Ask for a 30-day calendar organized by week with post type, topic, goal, and asset needed for each day. Specify a mix such as 50% value, 30% engagement, and 20% promotion, and request a table format. Feed it your real themes and promotions so the calendar reflects your business, not a generic template.

How do I stop AI-generated social posts from sounding generic?

Add constraints the model cannot ignore: your brand voice, audience, banned words, length limits, and examples. Ask for hooks under 15 words and forbid filler phrases like in today's fast-paced world. Include personal experiences or data only you know. The more specific the prompt, the less room the model has to produce average content.

Which platforms work best with ChatGPT prompts?

ChatGPT excels at text-heavy platforms like LinkedIn, X, and Facebook, and at captions, scripts, and plans for Instagram and TikTok. It cannot generate images or video, so pair it with design tools for visuals. Use platform-specific formats such as carousels for Instagram, thought leadership for LinkedIn, and short scripts for TikTok for the best results.

Can ChatGPT help analyze my social media analytics?

Yes, when you paste the numbers. ChatGPT can spot patterns in reach and engagement, flag underperforming formats, and suggest a testing plan. It cannot access your accounts, so export your metrics and paste them directly. Ask for three patterns, two weak formats, and one new content type to test in the next two weeks.
