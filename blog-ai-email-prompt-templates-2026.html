Why Your AI Emails Sound Generic Every Time

AI generates text by predicting what comes next based on everything you've told it. When you say "write me a cold email," the model has almost nothing to work with. It defaults to the most statistically likely email structure — which is, not coincidentally, the most generic one.

Three specific prompt mistakes cause this:

  • No recipient context. AI doesn't know who you're writing to, so it defaults to "safe" corporate language that offends no one but excites no one either.
  • No tone direction. Without explicit tone instructions, AI picks the median corporate tone — which reads as flat and forgettable.
  • No outcome clarity. Without knowing what specific action you want, AI writes to an vague "inform and hope" goal that rarely works.

The good news: fixing all three takes about 30 extra seconds in your prompt. The templates below handle it for you.

The 5-Second Fix Before You Use Any AI Email Prompt

Before you copy any template below, run this 30-second pre-step. It dramatically improves every AI email draft you write:

  1. Open your email thread or CRM note for this recipient
  2. Identify the one specific thing you want them to do after reading
  3. Ask: "What would make ME open and reply to this email?"
  4. Pick your tone keyword: direct, warm, confident, casual, technical

Now apply that to your prompt. The templates below are built to accept these details. Use them.

AI Prompt Templates for Email Writing — 15 Copy-Paste Examples

Cold Outreach Emails

Template 1: The Specific Cold Email

AI Prompt — copy and fill in brackets Write a cold email to [recipient's job title, e.g., "head of marketing at a 50-person e-commerce brand"]. Their biggest challenge right now is [specific problem, e.g., "generating qualified leads without increasing ad spend"]. I solve this by [what you do, e.g., "helping B2B brands build outbound sequences that actually book meetings"]. Tone: direct and confident — like someone who knows their stuff and respects the reader's time. Not salesy. Write only the email body. No subject line. Include a specific, low-effort ask at the end (something they can do in 30 seconds). Max 150 words.

Template 2: The Referral Email

AI Prompt Write a referral email from [your name], [your title] at [your company]. [Referrer's first name] suggested I reach out to you because [specific reason — what did the referrer say about this person?]. I want to [specific, concrete ask — e.g., "ask if you'd be open to a 20-minute call next week to compare notes on how you're handling customer onboarding"]. Tone: warm but not overly familiar. You're reaching out because someone vouched for you, not because you cold-mailed them. Max 120 words.

Template 3: The Follow-Up After No Response

AI Prompt Write a 4th follow-up email to [recipient] who has not responded to my previous 3 emails. My previous emails were about [topic]. I still believe this is relevant to them because [specific reason]. Tone: breezy and self-aware. Acknowledge you're aware this is a follow-up without apologizing. Give them an easy out. Include a "ps" that adds new value or a new angle on the original offer. Max 100 words. No subject line.

Customer Communication Templates

Template 4: Breaking Bad News (Refund/Delay/Error)

AI Prompt Write an email to a customer informing them that [the problem, e.g., "their order has been delayed by 5-7 business days due to a supplier issue"]. The tone should be: honest, direct, take full responsibility without groveling. Show genuine care without over-apologizing. Give them a concrete solution or next step. Do NOT use: "we apologize for any inconvenience" (it's hollow), "please bear with us", or "we're committed to excellence." Max 130 words.

Template 5: Welcoming a New Customer

AI Prompt Write a welcome email for a new customer who just signed up for [your product/service]. Their name is [name] and they're using it to [specific use case — what did they say they'd use it for?]. Tone: warm, human, slightly excited. Not corporate-template excited. Like a knowledgeable friend who genuinely thinks they'll get a lot out of this. Include one specific "get started" action they should take in the next 24 hours. Max 140 words.

Template 6: Post-Purchase Check-In

AI Prompt Write an email to [customer name] who purchased [product/service] [timeframe, e.g., "3 weeks ago"]. This is a genuine check-in — not a sales email. Ask them one specific question about their experience: [e.g., "what's the main thing you're using it for that you couldn't do before?"]. Tone: curious and human. Show you actually care about their outcome, not just NPS scores. Include a very low-key offer to help if they're having trouble (no hard upsell). Max 120 words.

Internal Communication Templates

Template 7: The Status Update Email

AI Prompt Write a project status update email to [stakeholder name or team]. Here's what happened this week: [2-3 bullet points of actual accomplishments]. Here's what's blocking us: [specific blocker]. Here's the plan for next week: [1-2 priorities]. Tone: confident and direct. Assume the reader is busy and smart. Lead with the bottom line. No preamble like "I hope this email finds you well." Max 160 words.

Template 8: The Meeting Decline with Alternative

AI Prompt Write an email declining a meeting request from [person] because [specific reason, e.g., "the agenda is too vague for me to prepare, or I'd be redundant to the conversation"]. Offer a specific alternative: either a shorter call focused on a specific question, an email exchange that could replace the meeting, or a meeting at a different time with a clearer agenda. Tone: polite but assertive. You're doing them a favor by being direct about this. Don't apologize excessively. Max 100 words.

Email Subject Lines That Work

Your subject line is the most important part of the email — and AI writes terrible ones by default. Use this prompt to generate 5 strong subject lines:

AI Prompt — Subject Line Generator Generate 5 email subject lines for an email about [what the email is about]. The recipient is [recipient description — their role, company size, or situation]. I want them to open this instead of ignoring it. Rules: - Max 50 characters each - 3 out of 5 should create curiosity, not describe the content - 2 out of 5 should be ultra-specific and benefit-driven - Do NOT use: "Quick question," "Quick chat," "Idea for you," or anything you could find in a generic email swipe file

How to Stop AI Emails From Sounding Robotic

Even with good prompts, AI has specific patterns that make it sound artificial. Here's what to explicitly tell AI to avoid:

  • "Do not start sentences with 'It is important to note that...'"
  • "Avoid the phrase 'I hope this email finds you well'"
  • "Do not use exclamation points more than once per email"
  • "Remove all passive voice sentences"
  • "Cut every sentence that could apply to any company in any industry"

Add these as a closing instruction to any email prompt:

Anti-robot closing instruction After writing the email, delete any sentence that meets ANY of these criteria: 1. Could apply to any company in any industry 2. Contains "leverage," "synergy," "circle back," or "touch base" 3. Starts with "I hope this email finds you" 4. Uses more than one exclamation point 5. Contains a phrase you've seen in more than three other AI-written emails

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does AI always give me generic email drafts?

AI gives generic emails because your prompt is too vague. Saying "write me a cold email" gives AI nothing to work with. The fix is to include your recipient's situation, what you want them to do, and the specific outcome you need — more context equals less generic output.

How do I make AI sound more human in emails?

Add a voice descriptor to your prompt: "Write this in a direct, slightly irreverent tone — like a smart friend who respects the reader's time." Also add "Avoid: corporate buzzwords, passive voice, and filler phrases." This cuts about 70% of robotic-sounding language.

What information should I include in an AI email prompt?

Include: who you're writing to, what problem you solve for them, the one specific action you want them to take, why you're different from alternatives, and the tone that matches your brand. The more specific the input, the less generic the output.

Can I use AI prompts for customer service emails?

Yes — and it's one of the highest-value use cases. Customer service prompts need: the customer's exact issue, their tier/status, your return/exchange/credit policy, and the desired emotional outcome (calm them down, surprise them with helpfulness).
