Postiz Alternatives: 7 Best Options for 2026

Published November 5, 2026 · 11 min read · Pricing verified November 2026

Postiz alternatives are the first search result for anyone who has actually run Postiz and hit a wall. The reason people hunt for an alternative is rarely that Postiz is bad — it is very good — but that something specific does not fit: a channel you need is missing, a server you do not want to maintain, a team that needs approvals, or a bill that grows every time you connect another account. This guide compares the seven realistic alternatives on price, platform coverage, AI and self-hosting, and tells you which one to pick. If you want to test the baseline first, you can try Postiz free for seven days and connect a real channel in about two minutes.

Why people look for Postiz alternatives at all

Postiz is an open-source (AGPL-3.0) social media scheduler from Gitroom that publishes to 30+ networks — X, LinkedIn, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Reddit, Telegram, Discord, Mastodon, Bluesky, Google Business, WordPress and more — from a single visual calendar. The repository at github.com/gitroomhq/postiz-app is actively developed and sits around 41,000 stars, so this is not a dying project you are escaping. It is a tool many people genuinely like.

But four things reliably push people off:

  • Operations. Self-hosting means Docker, a Postgres database, Redis, and a large set of environment variables. The official Docker documentation is honest about this: Postiz needs "a LOT of environment variables."
  • Channel gaps. The 30+ network list is impressive until you count what you actually publish to. A few smaller networks and niche formats are not supported.
  • Price at scale. Cloud plans count connected channels: Standard $29/mo for 5, Team $39 for 10, Pro $49 for 30, Ultimate $99 for 100. Unlimited posts, but channels are the unit.
  • Workflow gaps. There is no unified social inbox for replying to comments across platforms, and approval flows are lighter than enterprise tools.

Key takeaways

  • Best open-source alternative: Mixpost. One-time license ($299 Pro), no monthly fee, cleaner interface, posting queue, approvals, API, MCP server and webhooks — but its free Lite tier only publishes to Facebook, Mastodon and X.
  • Best cheap hosted alternative: Buffer. Free forever with 3 channels and 10 scheduled posts per channel; the simplest queue on the market.
  • Best analytics alternative: Metricool. Reporting, competitor tracking and ad dashboards, with X billed as a separate add-on on paid plans.
  • Best for enterprise workflow: Later or Hootsuite. Per-seat pricing and real approval, inbox and reporting layers — and the highest bills.
  • Best for developers: n8n or Activepieces. A general automation platform used as a scheduler, free and self-hostable, but you build the posting logic yourself.
  • Most people should not switch. If you publish to many channels and want AI agents in the loop, Postiz still leads on reach. See our Postiz vs Buffer breakdown and open-source scheduler guide first.

1. Mixpost — the best open-source alternative

Mixpost is the closest architectural cousin to Postiz: a Laravel-based, self-hostable social media manager from Inovector with no subscription model. The commercial model is unusual and worth understanding — you pay once. The free Lite edition gives you the calendar, media library, post versions, labels, basic analytics and publishing to Facebook Pages, Mastodon profiles and X profiles. The Pro license is $299 one-time (or $1,199 for Enterprise with white-label branding, subscription management and a customer dashboard), and it unlocks every platform, multi-tenant workspaces, advanced analytics, an engagement inbox, AI assistant, posting schedule/queue, approval flow, hashtag manager, post templates, API, MCP server and webhooks.

Where Mixpost beats Postiz: the interface is more polished, the license is a one-time cost rather than a subscription, and a perpetual fallback license means you keep the version you buy. Where it loses: the free tier publishes to three networks, so you need Pro for real coverage, and the AI image generation, AI video and video-clipping features are built deeper into Postiz. If you want the full picture, read the Mixpost pricing page and our Postiz self-hosting guide side by side.

2. Buffer — best cheap hosted alternative

Buffer remains the least complicated scheduler with a genuinely usable free tier: 3 channels, 10 scheduled posts per channel that refill as they publish, one user, AI Assistant, Insights and API access. Paid tiers start around $5 and $10 per month. It is also the one commercial scheduler where the free tier is not a demo — three channels is enough for a lot of one-person businesses. Our Postiz vs Buffer in 2026 analysis covers the price-per-channel math in detail. Buffer wins on polish and zero setup; it loses on breadth (a channel list that is mainstream-only), on self-hosting (there is none), and on agent integration depth.

3. Later — best for Instagram-first creators

Later's whole product is a visual drag-and-drop planner for Instagram and TikTok, and it is excellent at previewing how a grid will look before anything publishes. If your business is a single brand with a single Instagram, Later is often the right product at a lower price than enterprise suites. The Postiz vs Later comparison covers where Later's per-seat pricing and creator-first bias diverge from Postiz's channel-first model.

4. Metricool — best when analytics is the job

Metricool is a reporting platform that also schedules. It excels at competitor tracking, paid-ads dashboards across Meta, Google and TikTok, and PDF/PPT/Looker exports. The free plan covers one brand with 20 scheduled posts a month and no LinkedIn or X. Paid plans start near $20 per month and bill X as a separate per-account add-on, which is the number that catches agencies out. See Postiz vs Metricool for the brand-versus-channel pricing mismatch.

5. Hootsuite — best when you need the full enterprise layer

Hootsuite sells the whole operating system: publishing, a social inbox for customer care, approval workflows, benchmarking, listening and employee advocacy. The cost is the differentiator — Standard is $99 per month and Advanced is $399 per month, both far above anything else on this list. Choose Hootsuite when a social inbox and compliance-grade reporting are non-negotiable and the budget exists. If the budget does not exist, our three-way comparison shows the arithmetic.

6. n8n or Activepieces — best for developers

A general automation platform can schedule social posts. You wire a webhook or RSS feed into a node, call the Postiz or Buffer API, and get a full publishing pipeline with retry logic, logging and branching for free. It is genuinely unbeatable for one or two channels or for a content pipeline that already lives in n8n. It is also genuinely bad if you want a calendar UI — you have built the calendar yourself. Our Postiz and n8n workflow guide shows the wiring; the Postiz API guide covers the endpoints.

7. Staying on Postiz — the option most people skip

For a large share of searches, the honest answer is that Postiz is still the best of the alternatives on breadth and agent support. Thirty-plus networks including Reddit, Discord, Telegram, WordPress and Google Business is a list no hosted tool matches. The cloud tier starts at $29/month with unlimited posts, and every paid plan includes the public REST API, CLI, MCP server and webhooks, so Claude, ChatGPT or Cursor can drive publishing while a human approves in the calendar. If you are hesitating because of one missing feature, check whether a channel-specific workaround exists before you migrate 200 queued posts.

Postiz vs Mixpost: the comparison that actually decides most migrations

If your reason for looking at alternatives is cost predictability, this is the only head-to-head that matters, because both tools can be self-hosted indefinitely.

Stay on Postiz when

You publish to more than 10 networks, you want AI image generation, AI video and video clipping built in, you want an agent (Claude, ChatGPT, Codex) driving posts through MCP, or you want a public REST API with OAuth2 on every plan including the cheapest one. Cost: $0 self-hosted, or $29/mo cloud.

Switch to Mixpost when

You want a one-time license instead of a subscription, you value UI polish, you need multi-tenant white-label reselling for clients, you need an engagement inbox, or you simply do not want to operate a Node.js and Postgres stack. Cost: $299 once, or free Lite for 3 networks.

How to migrate without losing your schedule

There is no universal calendar export that every scheduler reads, so treat migration as a rebuild with a safety net rather than a one-click import.

  1. Export first. Pull your Postiz calendar and post library into a spreadsheet while Postiz is still live. Note every recurring post, every posting set, and every per-platform customization (different captions, different times, first comments).
  2. Reconnect accounts in the new tool. OAuth connections do not transfer. Revoking and re-authorising each channel is the tedious part and takes longer than the import.
  3. Recreate structure, not posts. In Mixpost use post templates and the posting schedule/queue; in Buffer use saved templates; in Later use repeatable posts. Rebuilding one template beats re-entering 150 posts.
  4. Run both in parallel for one full cycle. Publish one week on the old tool and the new one. Write down what you miss — that list is the real feature gap, and it tells you whether the migration was worth it.
  5. Cancel last. Keep the old subscription alive through the parallel cycle and one week of clean publishing on the new tool.

How to choose in five questions

  • Do you need to run it yourself? Yes → Mixpost or Postiz. No → Buffer, Later or Metricool.
  • How many networks do you publish to? More than 10, or anything outside the mainstream (Reddit, Discord, Telegram, Bluesky, Mastodon) → Postiz. Under 10 mainstream channels → Buffer or Later.
  • Is agent-driven publishing the goal? Yes → Postiz (MCP + CLI + API on every plan) or Mixpost Pro (MCP included).
  • Do you need a social inbox or comment replies? Yes → Hootsuite or Mixpost Pro.
  • Does the bill scale with you? If you want a fixed one-off cost → Mixpost. If you want zero software cost and one or two channels → n8n self-hosted.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best alternative to Postiz in 2026?

Mixpost is the closest open-source alternative to Postiz. It runs on your own server, has no monthly fee, and its $299 Pro license unlocks every platform, a posting queue, approvals, API, MCP server and webhooks. Buffer is the best alternative if you want a polished hosted product with a usable free plan, and Metricool wins on analytics depth rather than publishing breadth.

Is Mixpost really better than Postiz for self-hosting?

It depends on what you need. Mixpost has the cleaner interface and a one-time license with no recurring fee, but its free Lite edition only publishes to Facebook Pages, Mastodon and X. Postiz publishes to 30+ networks in the free self-hosted edition and ships a built-in picture editor, AI video clipping and agent integrations, so most teams should stay with Postiz and only switch for the interface or the license economics.

Can I migrate my scheduled posts from Postiz to another tool?

Yes, but expect manual work. No scheduler exports a portable calendar format that every other tool reads cleanly, so the practical route is to export your Postiz calendar, recreate recurring posts as posting sets or templates in the new tool, run both side by side for one publishing cycle, then cancel the old subscription once the new tool completes a full week without gaps.

Why do people look for Postiz alternatives at all?

The four recurring reasons are operational friction, missing channels, pricing at scale, and feature gaps. Self-hosting Postiz needs environment variables and Docker, some niche networks are missing, channel-based pricing grows with every account, and there is no built-in social inbox for comment replies. Which of these hurt you determines which alternative actually fixes the problem.

What is the cheapest Postiz alternative?

Free self-hosted options are cheapest. Mixpost Lite and Postiz both run at zero software cost on a small VPS, and n8n or Activepieces can act as a scheduler for one or two channels. Buffer's free plan is the cheapest hosted option with three channels and ten scheduled posts per channel, while Hootsuite's cheapest paid tier is far higher than any of these.

Is there a Postiz alternative with an MCP server for AI agents?

Yes. Mixpost Pro includes an MCP server, and hosted tools such as PostEverywhere and Post Bridge ship native MCP endpoints for agent-driven scheduling. Postiz includes an MCP server, CLI and public REST API on every paid plan, so if agent integration is the goal you may not need to switch at all.
