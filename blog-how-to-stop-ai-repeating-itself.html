How to Stop AI from Repeating Itself (The Two Settings Nobody Explains)

Published September 29, 2026 · 8 min read · Free Chrome Extension

You've seen it before: You ask AI to write something — an email, a blog post, a report — and it delivers three paragraphs that say essentially the same thing with slightly different words. The intro, the middle, and the conclusion all sound like variations of each other. So you ask it to fix it. It gives you another variation of the same three paragraphs.

You start to think you're bad at prompting. But the real problem is two settings you've probably never heard of: frequency penalty and presence penalty. Most AI tutorials barely mention them. This guide explains exactly what they do — and how to use them to get AI outputs that actually feel fresh.

The Repetition Problem Is a Settings Problem

If you've ever used an AI for anything beyond a quick one-off question, you've hit this wall. You ask for five ideas and get five versions of the same idea. You ask for a 500-word summary and get 500 words where every paragraph starts with some variation of "Additionally," "Furthermore," and "In conclusion." You ask for a list and the AI cyclizes through the same three items with slightly different framing.

Most people assume this is a prompt problem. It might be. But often, it's a settings problem. Specifically, it's a frequency penalty and presence penalty problem.

Most AI platforms set these to zero by default — meaning the AI has no instruction to avoid repeating itself. And language models, by design, tend toward the most statistically likely outputs, which are often the most commonly repeated phrases from their training data.

The Two Settings That Fix Repetition

Frequency Penalty: "Don't Repeat Words You've Already Used"

Frequency penalty is the more powerful of the two. When set above zero, it penalizes the AI for using words it has already used heavily in the conversation. The higher the number, the more the AI is pushed to find different ways to say the same thing.

❌ Frequency Penalty: 0

"Our solution is innovative, cutting-edge, and revolutionary. It provides innovative features with an innovative approach..."

✓ Frequency Penalty: 1.0

"Our platform redefines what's possible — combining a streamlined interface with measurable outcomes that set it apart from legacy tools."

The default of zero means the AI can repeat itself freely. A frequency penalty of 0.5–1.0 forces the AI to reach for synonyms, varied sentence structures, and fresh angles. Any higher than 1.5 and the output can become incoherent — the AI is so desperate to avoid repeats that it starts constructing grammatically strange sentences.

Presence Penalty: "Don't Keep Talking About the Same Topic"

Presence penalty is subtler. Instead of counting word occurrences, it penalizes the AI for staying on the same topics. A high presence penalty pushes the AI to introduce new ideas and angles.

This sounds useful — and it can be, for brainstorming. But for structured tasks like writing an email, a report, or answering a specific question, a high presence penalty makes the AI sound scattered. It keeps pivoting to new points before fully developing the ones you asked for.

❌ Presence Penalty: 2.0 (too high)

You ask for three benefits of a product. The AI gives you one benefit, then pivots to a tangent about pricing, then starts talking about the company's history, then勉强 gets back to benefit two.

✓ Presence Penalty: 0.3 (moderate)

You ask for three benefits. The AI gives you three well-developed benefits, each with a clear explanation and a specific example.

Why Default Settings Produce Generic, Repetitive Output

Here's what happens when you use AI with all default settings — which is what most people do:

  • Temperature at 0.7+ → random enough to be unpredictable, but not guided toward any specific goal
  • Frequency penalty at 0 → no penalty for repeating the same words, phrases, and structures
  • Presence penalty at 0 → no push to develop new angles or introduce fresh points

Result: AI generates text using the most statistically common patterns from its training data. These patterns are, by definition, generic. The most common transitions are "Furthermore," "Additionally," "In conclusion." The most common sentence structures are simple subject-verb-object. The most common phrases are the ones you've heard in a thousand corporate emails.

How to Actually Change These Settings

Here's the catch: most AI platforms don't give you direct access to frequency and presence penalty. ChatGPT's standard interface hides these settings. You can find them in:

  • ChatGPT: API settings (via Playground or API), not in the standard chat interface
  • Claude: Available in the API; the web interface uses fixed defaults
  • Gemini: Limited direct control in the standard UI
  • Perplexity: Pro users have some parameter access

This is exactly why most people don't know these settings exist — the consumer-facing interfaces abstract them away. But you can influence AI output behavior through your prompts and by using tools that apply these parameters for you.

The Prompt Workaround (When You Can't Access Settings)

When you can't directly adjust frequency and presence penalty, work around it with your prompt structure:

Prompt to reduce repetition (use this structure) "Write a blog post about time management." "Write a 600-word blog post about time management for remote workers. Requirements: - Use each of these three points ONCE only: Pomodoro technique, time blocking, task batching - Do not repeat any phrase more than once - Each paragraph should introduce a distinct sub-point not covered in other paragraphs - End with one specific action the reader can take today"

Explicitly telling the AI what not to repeat, and giving it a clear structure to follow, compensates partially for the lack of direct parameter control.

Why This Keeps Happening: The Model's Perspective

Understanding why AI repeats helps you fix it permanently. Language models generate text one word at a time, predicting each token based on probability. At each step, the model has no memory of what words it used ten turns ago — it only knows what's in the current context window.

So when you see the AI repeating "Furthermore," it's because that word has a high probability in that position given the training data — not because the AI decided to be boring. Frequency penalty addresses this by effectively lowering the probability of words the model has already used heavily in this conversation.

In longer conversations, the repetition problem compounds: as the context fills up with the AI's own previous outputs, the model starts sampling from a pool that's increasingly composed of its own generated text — leading to the kind of degenerate repetition patterns that users sometimes see in very long chats.

The Fix Is Simpler Than You Think

Before you spend twenty minutes rewording your prompt for the fifth time, try this:

  1. Set frequency penalty to 0.5–1.0 (this alone fixes most repetition)
  2. Keep presence penalty at 0.0–0.3 unless you're actively brainstorming
  3. Use temperature 0.3–0.5 for structured tasks where you want focused, coherent output
  4. Give the AI a structure — numbered lists, section headers, explicit instructions about what to cover and what to avoid

Most of the frustration people feel with AI repetition comes from not knowing these settings exist. Once you do, you can fix it in thirty seconds rather than fighting through five rewrites that each sound the same as the last.

Key Takeaways

  • Frequency penalty is your primary fix for repetitive AI output — set it to 0.5–1.0 for varied vocabulary and phrasing
  • Presence penalty keeps AI on-topic — keep it low (0.0–0.3) for structured tasks; raise it only for open brainstorming
  • Default settings produce default (generic, repetitive) output — the platform defaults are designed for conversational chat, not structured content creation
  • Prompt structure compensates when you can't access settings directly — tell the AI explicitly what to cover, in what order, and what to avoid repeating

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does AI keep repeating the same words and phrases?

AI repeats itself primarily because of how language models generate text — they calculate the probability of every possible next word, and without any penalty for repetition, they naturally gravitate toward high-frequency patterns they've seen in training data. The fix is to increase the frequency penalty setting (0.5–1.0) which penalizes words the AI has already used heavily in the conversation.

What is frequency penalty in AI?

Frequency penalty tells the AI to avoid repeating words it has already used a lot in the conversation. A higher frequency penalty (1.0 or more) makes the AI reach for different vocabulary and phrasing. Set it to 0.0 for tasks where repetition is acceptable or desirable, and 0.5–1.0+ when generating longer content where you want varied language.

What is presence penalty in AI?

Presence penalty encourages the AI to introduce new topics rather than staying focused on the same subject. A higher presence penalty (1.0+) makes the AI pivot to new angles and topics, which is useful for brainstorming but harmful for structured, focused tasks. For most practical work, keep presence penalty at 0.0 to 0.5.

Why does AI loop back to the same talking points?

AI loops back to the same points because it has no built-in mechanism to track what it's already said — each token prediction is made independently. To prevent looping, use a higher frequency penalty (1.0+) to discourage repeated words, and consider breaking long tasks into smaller prompts with explicit instructions not to repeat previous points.

What's the best frequency penalty for blog posts?

For blog posts and long-form content, a frequency penalty of 0.5–1.0 works well to keep language varied and prevent repetitive phrase patterns. Combine this with a temperature of 0.5–0.7 for creative but focused writing. If the AI starts jumping between too many unrelated topics, lower the presence penalty to 0.0–0.3.

How do I stop AI from repeating itself in conversations?

To stop AI repetition in conversations: (1) Increase frequency penalty to 0.5–1.0, (2) If the AI keeps changing topics, lower presence penalty to 0.0–0.3, (3) In longer conversations, explicitly tell the AI what you already covered and what you want next, (4) Consider using a prompt enhancer tool that structures your input to keep the AI focused.
