Why the Postiz vs Metricool comparison needs the real numbers

Most comparison pages for this pair get the pricing wrong in the same way: they quote the entry-plan headline and stop. That misses the two facts that actually decide the bill. First, Metricool prices by brand and charges a separate add-on for X/Twitter, so a five-brand agency publishing on X pays noticeably more than the sticker price. Second, Metricool has no self-hosted edition at any tier, while Postiz is AGPL-3.0 open source and free to run on your own VPS.

The other reason the comparison is worth doing carefully in 2026: Postiz has shifted from "open-source Buffer alternative" to an agentic scheduling platform. It runs a hosted MCP server, a CLI, and a public REST API, and agents like Claude, ChatGPT and Cursor can draft, schedule and publish through it while a human approves in the calendar. Metricool has an MCP server too, but its centre of gravity is reporting and brand management, not agent-driven publishing.

Key takeaways

  • Metricool wins on reporting: competitor tracking, paid-ads dashboards (Meta, Google, TikTok) and PDF/PPT/Looker Studio exports on top of network analytics.
  • Postiz wins on reach: 30+ channels versus roughly 10-11, including Reddit, Discord, Mastodon, Bluesky, Telegram and Google Business.
  • The X add-on is the pricing trap: Metricool bills roughly $10 per month for each connected X account on paid plans; Postiz includes X with no surcharge.
  • Postiz is the only one you can self-host — Docker, AGPL-3.0, and no per-seat pricing once a few people need to publish.
  • Both price by unit, but different units: Postiz counts connected channels (5 to 100), Metricool counts brands (1 to 50).

Channel counting is not brand counting

This trips people up constantly. In Metricool, a brand holds one profile per social network — one Instagram, one TikTok, one LinkedIn. If you run three Facebook pages for one client, that is three brands or one brand with an extra seat, and the pricing moves. In Postiz, every connected account is one channel against your plan limit, so three Facebook pages is three channels, and you can swap or disconnect channels at any time without losing content.

Analytics and reporting: where Metricool is genuinely better

I want to be honest about this section, because a lot of Postiz-adjacent content pretends the comparison is a clean sweep, and it is not. Metricool is a better analytics product. It reports across every network you connect, tracks up to 100 competitor profiles, unifies Meta, Google and TikTok ad campaigns in one dashboard, generates automated reports with AI analysis, and exports to PDF, PowerPoint and Looker Studio for client delivery. Its Data Studio connector lets a brand span multiple Google Business Profiles, which is a real operational need for multi-location businesses.

Postiz reports impressions, likes, comments, shares, reach and engagement rate per channel and per post in one dashboard, which is more than enough to answer "which format performs best". What it does not do is paid-ads reporting, competitor tracking, or the client-ready PDF/PPT export layer.

Pick Metricool if…

Reporting is your actual job. You run ads, you benchmark competitors, you send monthly client reports, or you manage 25+ brands and need per-brand collaborator permissions. Its free plan is also a genuinely useful free tier if you do not need X or LinkedIn.

Pick Postiz if…

Publishing is your actual job. You post to more than a handful of channels, you hit per-brand pricing tiers, you want your data on your own infrastructure, or you want an AI agent to draft and schedule content while you approve it in one calendar.

Self-hosting Postiz with Docker

Postiz is published on GitHub under the AGPL-3.0 license at gitroomhq/postiz-app, and the official Docker Compose self-hosting guide is tested on a 2 vCPU / 2 GB RAM Ubuntu VM. The honest caveat: "free" software is not "free" operations. You own the server, the database, the queue worker, backups, upgrades, and — the part people forget — you must create your own developer app on each social network and handle the API approvals yourself. Budget a couple of hours for the first setup and a few minutes per month after that.

How to migrate from Metricool to Postiz

Migration is mostly a data-shape problem, not a technical one, because both tools connect to social networks with official OAuth rather than by scraping.

  1. Export first. Pull your full content calendar and recurring-post schedule out of Metricool before you touch anything. Recurring posts are the part that gets lost.
  2. Connect the same accounts in Postiz. Reauthorising each network takes a couple of minutes per account. Expect a waiting period on review for some networks.
  3. Recreate recurring content as posting sets. Postiz posting sets are the equivalent of a Metricool recurring series — set the cadence once and it re-runs.
  4. Run both tools in parallel for one cycle. Two weeks of real publishing tells you what you actually miss. People are usually surprised by what does not break.
  5. Cancel Metricool last. Only once Postiz has published a full week without gaps. Keep the export file — you will want it for historical context.

If you run RSS-to-social automation, note that Postiz includes RSS auto-posting and repeated evergreen posts on every plan, so that use case does not force you into a higher tier.

Metricool is built for agencies: a brand is a client, collaborators get access to one brand only, and you can manage 50 brands on the Advanced plan.
