Why scheduling LinkedIn posts is a different problem

LinkedIn is not a forgiving publishing target. It has a 3,000-character limit on both profiles and Pages, it rejects media on thread parts, and it has no native concept of a carousel. Publishers learn this the hard way: a post that works fine on X gets truncated, a document upload gets rejected, and a multi-part thread silently loses its media.

Three structural quirks cause most of the friction, and each one changes what a good scheduling tool needs to do.

1. Carousels are PDFs, not a native format

A LinkedIn "carousel" is a document share. LinkedIn renders a PDF as a swipeable document, and there is no API that accepts a set of slides. Every scheduler therefore has to solve the same problem: combine your images into a PDF, then upload that PDF as a document. Most tools push this back onto you as a manual step. Postiz does the conversion automatically — attach two or more images and it builds the PDF and uploads it as a document share.

2. A profile and a Page are different channels

Postiz treats them as two separate providers, linkedin and linkedin-page, and each connected account consumes one channel from your plan. This matters for more than billing. Analytics are available for LinkedIn Pages but personal profiles expose far less through the official API, so profile-level performance data stays thin. If you build a reporting habit around Pages, do not expect the same numbers from a personal profile.

3. Comments are text-only

If you write a multi-part post, the extra parts become comments, and LinkedIn rejects media attached to them. Any tool promising to schedule a LinkedIn thread with images in the replies is going to fail at publish time rather than at save time.

What LinkedIn's own scheduler does (and does not do)

LinkedIn ships a native scheduler, and it is genuinely good for what it covers. You can schedule a post from roughly one hour ahead to three months in advance, on both personal profiles and company Pages, from the clock icon in the composer. It supports text, images, video, and document uploads, it is free, and it needs no third-party account. If you publish three posts a week on one LinkedIn account and never touch another network, the native scheduler is the correct answer.

How to schedule LinkedIn posts the manual way

Before paying for anything, it is worth knowing exactly what the manual workflow looks like, because that is the baseline you are trying to beat.

  1. Write the post. Keep it under 3,000 characters. Write a longer version for LinkedIn and a shorter one for X, rather than truncating one master copy.
  2. Build the carousel if you need one. Export your slides as images, combine them into a single PDF, and confirm the page size reads well on a phone.
  3. Open the LinkedIn composer. Paste the text, upload the PDF as a document.
  4. Check the timezone. LinkedIn schedules in UTC. If you are in a non-UTC region, the slot you pick is not the slot you get.
  5. Set the time and confirm. You get a range of about one hour to three months.
  6. Repeat for every other network. This is the step that quietly eats the time you were trying to save.

Steps one through five are maybe three minutes per post. Step six is where manual scheduling falls apart, and it is the reason people look for a tool at all.

Self-hosting: what the LinkedIn setup actually requires

This is where most self-hosted setups quietly break, so it is worth being precise. Connecting LinkedIn on your own instance requires your own LinkedIn developer app, and it needs three products enabled: Share on LinkedIn, the Advertising API, and Sign In with LinkedIn using OpenID Connect.

Under the Auth tab, the OAuth 2.0 scopes list must show every scope Postiz requests:

  • openid and profile — from OpenID Connect
  • w_member_social — from Share on LinkedIn, for personal profiles
  • r_basicprofile
  • rw_organization_admin, w_organization_social, r_organization_social — from the Advertising API, for Pages

Two warnings from the official provider documentation are worth repeating. First, request the Advertising API permissions and complete the request form, or you will not be able to refresh tokens and your channel will break when the access token expires. Second, the organization scopes are required for the regular LinkedIn provider too, not just the Page one — both providers share the same app and the same scope set.

If a scope is missing, LinkedIn does not fail the login. It issues a token without that scope, and Postiz rejects the connection with a NotEnoughScopes error. The Auth tab list is the first place to check.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I schedule LinkedIn posts for free?

Yes. LinkedIn's native scheduler is free and queues a post from about one hour to three months ahead, on personal profiles and company pages. It handles text, images, video, and document uploads. It does not build carousel PDFs, does not reuse content across other networks, and gives you no cross-platform calendar. For a few posts on LinkedIn alone, it is enough.

How far in advance can I schedule a LinkedIn post?

LinkedIn's native scheduler accepts posts from roughly one hour ahead to three months in advance. Third-party tools such as Postiz and Buffer generally allow much longer lead times, and Postiz's queue holds far more upcoming posts than LinkedIn's own interface. LinkedIn also runs its schedule in UTC, so a slot you pick locally can land hours away. Check the timezone label before confirming.

What is the difference between a LinkedIn profile post and a LinkedIn Page post?

A profile post publishes as the individual, and a Page post publishes as the organisation. In Postiz these are two separate providers, named linkedin and linkedin-page, and each connected account counts as one channel against your plan limit. The important operational difference is analytics: Postiz surfaces impressions, engagement, and reach for LinkedIn Pages, but personal profiles expose far less through the official API, so profile-level analytics stay thin or unavailable.

Can I schedule LinkedIn PDF carousels?

Yes, but not the way you might expect. A LinkedIn carousel is not a native feature. It is a document share: a PDF that LinkedIn renders as a swipeable document. Postiz does the conversion for you. Attach at least two images and Postiz combines them into a PDF and uploads it as a document share. A carousel needs two or more images, and LinkedIn comments are text-only.

Do I need a LinkedIn developer app to use Postiz?

Not on Postiz Cloud. The hosted version already has a LinkedIn application configured, so you click LinkedIn, sign in, pick your profile or Page, and the channel is connected. Self-hosting means creating your own LinkedIn app and requesting the Share on LinkedIn, Advertising API, and OpenID Connect products. Postiz also requests Advertising API permissions so it can refresh tokens long-term.

Does scheduling LinkedIn posts hurt engagement or reach?

No. Posting from an official API does not carry the reach penalty that aggressive automation or third-party browser extensions can. LinkedIn treats API-published content from a verified app the same as content published in the app. What matters more is consistency and the first hour of engagement. Scheduling ahead of your best posting window is normal professional practice, and using a scheduler does not change how the post ranks.
