Why AI Keeps Ignoring Your Formatting Instructions — And How to Fix It

Published October 2, 2026 · 7 min read · AI Prompting

You open ChatGPT. You type: "Write me three email subject lines — no longer than 50 characters, punchy, curiosity-driven. Just those three lines, nothing else." You hit enter. What comes back? A paragraph introduction, a disclaimer, a summary, three subject lines buried in the middle, and a friendly sign-off. You told it exactly what you wanted. It ignored every word. Sound familiar?

You are not imagining this. This is one of the most common complaints in AI prompting communities — and it has nothing to do with how smart the model is. It has everything to do with how you structured your instructions.

Why AI Ignores Your Format Instructions

Here is what is actually happening inside the model when you say "reply in JSON" or "use bullet points":

The AI processes your entire prompt as one continuous stream of information. It does not read it the way you read an email — with a subject line, a body, and a P.S. It reads it the way a river flows: everything at once, all mixed together. Every sentence competes for the model's attention.

When you bury a formatting instruction inside a longer prompt — especially in the middle or at the end — the model weighs it against everything else it has been trained to do. And more often than not, the default behavior (bullets, friendly tone, comprehensive answer) wins.

A user on Reddit described it perfectly: "I asked for JSON. It gave me a paragraph, then said 'here is the JSON:' and then put the JSON in a code block. It was like it didn't even read that part."

That is not a broken model. That is the model doing exactly what it was trained to do — produce the most natural, most complete-looking response it can. Formatting instructions buried in text are just... more text, as far as the model is concerned.

The 4 Reasons Your Formatting Instructions Fail

1. Position bias — instructions at the end get deprioritized

AI models process prompts sequentially. Early tokens get more attention because they set the context for everything that follows. Instructions at the end of a long prompt are treated as afterthoughts — helpful context, not hard rules. This is called position bias, and it is one of the most underestimated reasons formatting fails.

2. Competing priorities — you asked for too much at once

If your prompt contains a detailed task description, a list of constraints, tone guidance, audience parameters, AND a format instruction, the model tries to satisfy all of them simultaneously. When priorities are not ranked, the model defaults to what feels most 'complete' — usually a structured, bullet-pointed answer that covers every base.

3. Vague formatting language — "make it concise" means nothing to AI

Phrases like "keep it short", "just give me the facts", or "don't be so wordy" are interpretive. The model decides what counts as 'concise.' To an AI trained on comprehensive responses, three sentences might feel like being concise. To you, it feels like it ignored you.

4. No consequence — the model has no reason to obey

When you ask a human to do something with a format constraint, there is social accountability. When you ask AI the same way, it has no built-in reason to treat your format request as a constraint rather than a preference. Adding a consequence — "if you include anything not requested, I will ask you to redo it" — surprisingly works, because it frames the format as a success criterion rather than a suggestion.

How to Actually Get AI to Follow Your Format Instructions

The fix is not to use a longer prompt. It is to use a differently structured prompt. Here is the difference:

❌ The Prompt That Gets Ignored

Can you help me draft an email to my team about the quarterly results? I want to be transparent about where we fell short but also motivate them for next quarter. Keep it professional, direct, and no longer than 200 words. Just the email body, no subject line or sign-off.

✓ The Prompt That Gets Followed

Write a 200-word team email body about quarterly results. Format: plain prose paragraphs, no bullets, no headings, no lists. Tone: direct and honest about shortfalls, motivating at the end. Do not include a subject line or sign-off. If you include any formatting I did not request, redo it.

The second prompt is not longer. It is more structurally explicit. The format instruction comes first, it uses constraint language ("do not include"), and it adds a consequence. That combination is dramatically more effective.

The Hierarchy That Actually Works

Based on community research and prompting best practices, here is the order that gets the highest format-follow rate:

  1. Format instruction first — Put what you want at the very top of the prompt, before anything else.
  2. Be specific, not descriptive — "Three bullet points of no more than 10 words each" beats "keep it brief."
  3. Use constraint language — "Do not use X" is more effective than "try to avoid X."
  4. Add a consequence — "If you add anything I didn't ask for, I will ask you to redo it" works.
  5. Put your actual request last — Yes, the content request goes at the end. The format instruction comes first.

Format Problems by AI Type

Different AI models have different format habits. Knowing yours helps you write more targeted instructions:

  • ChatGPT — Over-uses bullet points and section headings by default. Explicitly say "no bullets, plain paragraphs only" if you want prose.
  • Claude — Tends toward thoroughness, often adding disclaimers and caveats. Add "be direct, no caveats" to override.
  • Gemini — Can be inconsistent with structured output. Use explicit format tags like [OUTPUT FORMAT: JSON] at the very top of your prompt.
  • Perplexity — Designed for research; defaults to sourced, citation-heavy answers. Say "no citations, no source links" to override.
  • Grok — More irreverent tone; may add commentary. Add "factual and neutral tone only" to constrain.

FAQ — People Also Ask

Why does AI ignore my formatting instructions?

AI weighs formatting instructions against every other part of your prompt. When format requirements are buried in a long prompt, the model treats them as gentle preferences rather than hard constraints, and emits whatever output structure seems most natural.

How do I make AI follow my format instructions every time?

Put formatting requirements at the very top of your prompt, in their own clear sentence. Be explicit about what you want — bullet points, plain prose, JSON, a specific word count — and add a consequence like "If you add anything not requested, I will ask you to redo it."

Why does AI keep giving me bullet points when I ask for paragraphs?

LLMs default to bullet lists because they are statistically easy to generate and feel 'complete.' Your paragraph instruction competes against the model's habit of producing structured, scannable output. State format first, and add "Do not use bullet points" if you need to be explicit.

Does putting instructions at the start or end of a prompt matter?

Yes — research shows instructions at the very start of a prompt get the highest follow rate because the model processes them as directives before it begins generating. Instructions buried mid-prompt or at the end are treated as context, not commands.
