Why Spreadsheets Break Down at Scale

Spreadsheets worked fine when you were posting to two platforms with a handful of accounts. But as your social media operation grows, the cracks appear fast:

  • No auto-publishing — someone has to be online to copy-paste content at the right time.
  • No cross-platform visibility — a TikTok post and a LinkedIn post on the same day are in separate tabs, if they're tracked at all.
  • Approval chaos — email chains and Slack DMs pile up trying to get a post reviewed before it goes live.
  • No analytics connection — the spreadsheet tracks what you planned, not what actually performed.

Postiz was built to replace exactly this workflow. Its queue system turns your content plan into an automated publishing pipeline — no human intervention required at posting time.

What Is a Postiz Queue?

A queue in Postiz is a scheduled pipeline of posts organized by platform, time slot, and publication date. When you create a post in Postiz, you don't publish it immediately — you add it to a queue for a specific date and time. Postiz handles the rest:

  • Queue-based publishing — posts go live automatically at the scheduled time, even if your computer is off.
  • Platform-specific formatting — Postiz automatically adjusts aspect ratios, caption lengths, and hashtag formats for each network.
  • Queue rotation — reoccurring content (weekly tips, Monday motivation posts) can be set to recycle automatically without re-entering it manually.
  • Bulk CSV import — upload a spreadsheet of posts and Postiz maps it to your queues in seconds.

The Postiz Content Calendar: Your Full Month at a Glance

Postiz's calendar view is where the system pays off. Instead of scrolling through a spreadsheet, you see your entire month's content organized visually:

  • Drag-and-drop rescheduling — grab a post and move it to a different slot with one drag. Perfect for when a campaign date changes.
  • Color-coding by platform — assign colors to Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn, or any custom category so gaps are visible instantly.
  • Gap detection — if your Tuesday slots are empty, you'll see the blank spaces and can fill them before the week arrives.
  • Filter by team member — see exactly what each person on your team is responsible for publishing.

Team Collaboration: Roles, Approvals, and Publishing Rights

Postiz was built for teams, not just solo creators. The permission system lets you control exactly who can create, edit, approve, and publish content:

  • Admin role — full access to all queues, settings, team management, and billing.
  • Editor role — create and schedule posts but cannot change queue settings or team permissions.
  • Approver role — review posts before they enter the queue. Useful for agencies that need client sign-off before publishing.
  • Viewer role — read-only access to the calendar. Good for stakeholders who need visibility without edit power.

For social media managers running client accounts, the approver role is a workflow game-changer. Instead of screenshotting a draft and sending it via email, the client reviews inside Postiz, approves with one click, and the post enters the queue automatically.

How to Set Up Your First Postiz Queue

Getting started with queues takes about 15 minutes. Here's the high-level flow:

Step 1 — Connect Your Platforms Navigate to Settings → Integrations and connect each social account via OAuth. Postiz uses each platform's official API, so there are no scraping risks.
Step 2 — Create a Queue Go to Queues → New Queue. Name it (e.g., "Weekly Tips"), select the platforms, and set your posting frequency — daily, specific days, or intervals.
Step 3 — Build Your Content Use the composer to create posts. Assign each post to a queue and set the date/time. You can bulk-create posts for the whole week in one sitting.
Step 4 — Approve and Launch If your team uses approvals, submit posts for review. Once approved, they enter the queue automatically and Postiz publishes at the scheduled time.
