What Are YouTube Shorts (vs. Regular YouTube Videos)?

YouTube Shorts are vertical videos up to 60 seconds long, designed for mobile-first consumption and discovered through YouTube's Shorts tab. They're a fundamentally different format than standard YouTube videos:

  • Format: 9:16 vertical (vs. 16:9 horizontal for standard videos)
  • Length: Up to 60 seconds (standard videos go up to 12 hours)
  • Upload method: Via YouTube's Content Posting API — no re-upload required on mobile
  • Discovery: Shorts feed vs. search/subscription feed
  • Monetization: Shorts Fund + Overlay ads (vs. full pre-roll + display ads)

If you're creating both long-form content and Shorts, managing them from separate workflows is a time sink. Postiz brings both into one visual calendar.

Scheduling YouTube Shorts: Step by Step

Step 1: Connect Your YouTube Channel

In Postiz, go to Settings → Channels → Add Channel and select YouTube. Postiz uses Google's official OAuth flow — you'll authorize Postiz to post to your channel. No app credentials needed on your end.

What happens during OAuth 1. Postiz redirects to Google sign-in 2. You grant "Manage your YouTube videos" permission 3. Google returns an OAuth token to Postiz 4. Postiz can now upload on your behalf — you stay in control

Step 2: Create a New Short Post

Click + New Post and choose YouTube Shorts as the channel. Upload your vertical video file (MP4, MOV, up to 60 seconds). Postiz detects the 9:16 aspect ratio and marks it as a Short automatically.

Step 3: Add Your Caption and Tags

Postiz's AI copilot writes your Short caption based on your video topic. It also suggests:

  • Trending hashtag combinations for the Shorts algorithm
  • Platform-specific hook phrases (the first 3 seconds of caption text)
  • Optimal posting times based on your audience's time zones

Step 4: Schedule or Queue

Choose a publish date and time, or add the Short to your recurring queue. The queue mode automatically pulls from your pre-approved content list and publishes on your configured schedule — ideal for creators who batch-record Shorts in one session.

Best Practices for YouTube Shorts Scheduling in 2026

Post at Peak Shorts Times

YouTube Shorts perform best when posted between 6pm–9pm local time on weekdays and 9am–12pm on weekends. Postiz's analytics show you when your specific audience is most active — use that data to fine-tune your Shorts schedule rather than guessing.

Don't Post Shorts and Long-Form Simultaneously

If you upload a Short and a standard video at the same time to the same channel, YouTube's algorithm may deprioritize one to avoid cannibalization. Postiz's calendar view helps you spot these conflicts before they happen.

Repurpose Long-Form Clips as Shorts

The most efficient Shorts strategy: record a longer video, then use Postiz's queue to automatically pull clips (via n8n or similar automation) and schedule them as Shorts throughout the week. This is the workflow most viral Shorts creators use.

Keep Your Shorts Calendar Ahead by 2 Weeks

Maintain a buffer of at least 14 days of scheduled Shorts. This gives you time to review analytics, swap underperforming content, and stay consistent even during busy weeks when you can't record new material.
