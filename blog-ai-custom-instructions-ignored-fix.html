Why Custom Instructions Fail: The Real Reason

Custom Instructions sound like a permanent solution. Set them once, and your AI remembers who you are across every conversation. But here's what the product pages don't tell you: Custom Instructions are treated as suggestions, not commands.

The core problem is a conflict baked into how modern language models work:

  • RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) trains AI to be helpful above all else. When the model decides your instruction conflicts with what it considers a more helpful response, it overrides you — every time.
  • System prompts from the AI company sit above your Custom Instructions in priority. Updates to the underlying model can quietly override or ignore user-set instructions without warning.
  • Context window interference: when your current conversation has a lot of back-and-forth, your Custom Instructions can get diluted as the model allocates attention to recent messages over persistent preferences.

One user on r/ClaudeAI put it bluntly: "I spent $2k and the hard lesson is that markdown instructions don't work." That's not hyperbole — it's a real experience from paying users who expected the feature to work as advertised.

The "Recapping" Technique: What Actually Works

The workaround that consistently works in developer communities and power-user forums is something called the recapping technique. Instead of relying on Custom Instructions as your single source of truth, you open every conversation by restating your core requirements.

This sounds tedious — but it's the only approach that reliably works because it places your instructions directly in the active context window, where the model has to pay attention to them.

❌ Relying Only on Custom Instructions

Set it once, forget it. AI ignores it when it conflicts with RLHF priorities. You get generic responses and have to re-instruct every single chat.

✓ Recapping at the Start of Every Session

Open with a 2-3 sentence summary of your key preferences. AI reads it fresh every time. Your requirements stay in the active context window. Consistent results follow.

How to Write a Recap Prompt

A good recap prompt covers four things in 2-3 sentences:

  1. Your role or audience — who this is for
  2. Tone and format — how you want it written
  3. What to avoid — things that waste your time
  4. Your goal — what you're trying to accomplish
Recap Prompt — Copy and Paste At the start of every chat, paste this: "I need you to help me as a senior software engineer working on production systems. Write in a direct, technical tone — no beginner explanations, no bullet points unless I ask for them, and no disclaimers. Avoid generic advice. My goal today is [state it]. Let's get to it."

You can adapt this to any role or use case. The key is that it goes at the top of every conversation, not just when you set up Custom Instructions.

Why the Same Prompt Gives Different Answers Every Time

A related problem users hit constantly: you ask the same question on different days and get completely different answers. That's not a bug — it's a feature of how LLMs generate text.

Each response is sampled from a probability distribution. Temperature settings, token limits, and model version updates all shift that distribution over time. Without explicit constraints in your prompt, the model is optimised for variety, not consistency.

The recapping technique helps here too — by specifying output format and constraints every time, you narrow the model's creative space and get closer to reproducible results.

What About Projects and Persistent Context Files?

Claude's Projects feature and similar persistent context tools are the next evolution of this problem. Users report that even within a Project — where you've carefully set up instructions — a new conversation thread can feel like talking to a stranger who forgot everything you agreed on.

Power users have developed workarounds:

  • Keep a PROJECT.md or STATE.md file in every project
  • Drop its contents into the first message of every new conversation thread
  • Specifically reference "as stated in our PROJECT.md" to push the model back to your context

This is the community's most upvoted workaround for AI context loss — and it's exactly why the recapping technique generalises so well.

FAQ: Custom Instructions and AI Instruction-Following

Why does AI ignore my custom instructions?

AI ignores custom instructions primarily due to RLHF training, which optimises for perceived helpfulness over strict instruction-following. When your instruction conflicts with what the model considers a better response, it overrides your rules. Context limits and prompt interference can also dilute custom instructions over the course of a conversation.

Do custom instructions work on all AI chatbots?

Custom instructions are available on ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini, but each handles them differently. ChatGPT and Gemini call them Custom Instructions, while Claude uses Instructions and Styles. Not all AI platforms support them, and those that do don't all guarantee they'll be followed consistently.

How do I make AI actually follow my instructions?

Start every conversation with a recapping prompt that restates your key preferences and requirements before diving into the task. This pushes your instructions into the active context window where they're harder for the model to override. Pair this with specific output format requirements in each prompt rather than relying on Custom Instructions alone.

What is the recapping prompt technique?

The recapping technique involves opening every conversation with a summary of your most important preferences — tone, format, audience, things to avoid. By restating these at the start of each session, you refresh the model's memory of your instructions without relying on the Custom Instructions feature alone.
