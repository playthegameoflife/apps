Why Generic Prompts Produce Generic Resumes

Ask ChatGPT to write a resume and it assembles the average of every resume in its training data. That average is full of vague achievements, empty adjectives, and no numbers, because real numbers were stripped out of most public examples. The deeper problem is that the model does not know anything about you, so it fills the gaps with what sounds plausible. Those invented metrics are the fastest way to get a rejection or a painfully awkward interview.

Every useful ChatGPT resume prompt solves the same two problems: it gives the model your real facts, and it tells the model exactly how to use them. The prompts below follow one formula, which we call the context sandwich, because it is the same structure behind every good prompt in our guide to structuring AI prompts.

The Context Sandwich: A Formula for Every Job-Search Prompt

Instead of a one-line request, build prompts with five layers: role, facts, task, constraints, and output format. Role tells the model who to be; facts are your truth bank; task is the specific job to do; constraints are the rules it must obey, like never invent metrics; output format tells it exactly what to return. Fill the five layers once and every application becomes a copy-paste exercise.

Prompt 1 is the master template. Keep it saved and adapt it for each job:

Role: You are an expert resume writer who has worked with hiring
managers in [INDUSTRY] for 15 years. You never invent facts.

Facts (my truth bank — use ONLY these):
- Current title: [YOUR TITLE] at [COMPANY], [START MONTH/YEAR]–present
- Previous role: [TITLE] at [COMPANY], [DATES]
- Tools and skills: [LIST]
- Real results I can prove: [NUMBER]% [METRIC] in [TIME], [OTHER PROOF]
- Things I cannot claim: anything not listed above

Task: Rewrite my resume summary and work-experience bullets so they
match this job description: [PASTE JOB DESCRIPTION].

Constraints:
- Use only facts from my truth bank. Never add numbers, titles, or
  employers I did not provide.
- Mirror the keywords in the job description without copying it.
- Active voice, strong verbs, no "responsible for" or "results-driven".

Output format: A 2-line summary, then each role with 3 bullet options,
then a one-line note on the keyword matches you made.

ChatGPT Resume Prompts That Turn Bullets Into Proof

Recruiters scan a resume for about seven seconds, and what they look for in that time is evidence: what you did, how well, and with what measurable result. The prompts in this section force ChatGPT to convert responsibilities into proof. Copy the ones you need, fill in the brackets, and always keep your fact bank nearby.

Prompt 2 — Rewrite a weak bullet from your facts.

Here is a bullet from my resume: "[YOUR ORIGINAL BULLET]".
Rewrite it to show impact. Use my fact bank below, start with a strong
verb, and include one measurable result if my facts support it.
Never invent a number. Give me 3 versions ranked by strength.
Fact bank: [YOUR FACTS]

Prompt 3 — Turn a responsibility into three achievement bullets.

My role included "[RESPONSIBILITY]". Using only these facts,
[YOUR FACTS], write three achievement bullets for my resume.
Each bullet must start with an action verb and include a metric,
a time frame, or a scope detail that my facts support.

Prompt 4 — Quantify without fabricating.

I know these facts are true: [FACTS WITH ROUGH NUMBERS, e.g. "team of
4", "budget of $50K", "cut report time roughly in half"]. Turn them
into resume bullets with precise-sounding but honest numbers, and if a
number needs rounding, show it in parentheses so I can verify it.

Prompt 5 — Rewrite bullets for a career change.

I am moving from [OLD FIELD] to [NEW FIELD]. Rewrite my bullets so the
transferable skills (project management, client communication, data
analysis) are front and center, while keeping my job titles and dates
exact. Use only my fact bank: [FACTS].

Prompt 6 — Explain a resume gap honestly.

I have a gap from [MONTH/YEAR] to [MONTH/YEAR] because [REASON:
caregiving / layoff / education / health]. How should I present this
gap in my resume and in interviews? Give me one honest resume line and
three interview responses that do not apologize and do not over-explain.

ATS-Friendly Resume Prompts

Applicant tracking systems screen most resumes before a human sees them. They parse your text and look for keywords from the job description; they do not judge writing quality and they do not detect AI. That means the practical risks are formatting that does not parse and keywords that do not match. Applicant tracking systems are simply software that stores and filters applications, as the Wikipedia overview of applicant tracking systems explains, and the Society for Human Resource Management tracks how widely employers rely on them in hiring.

Prompt 7 — Extract the keywords that matter.

Here is a job description: [PASTE JD].
List the 15 keywords and phrases a recruiter or ATS would scan for
(hard skills, software, certifications, role-specific terms). Group
them as "must match", "should match", and "nice to have", and flag any
that I genuinely do not have so I do not stuff them in.

Prompt 8 — Weave keywords into your existing bullets.

My current bullets: [PASTE BULLETS].
Keywords from the job: [LIST FROM PROMPT 7].
Rewrite each bullet so the keywords appear naturally where I truly
used them. Do not add a keyword I cannot defend in an interview.
Show a before/after for each bullet.

Prompt 9 — Audit your resume like a parser.

Act as an ATS parser. Read my resume text: [PASTE RESUME].
Tell me: (1) any section headers or dates a parser might misread,
(2) missing standard sections, (3) keyword gaps versus this job
description [PASTE JD], and (4) whether my job title matches the
title in the posting. Give fixes for each issue.

Prompt 10 — Optimize the skills section.

My skills section is: [LIST]. The job requires: [REQUIREMENTS].
Reorder and regroup my skills to mirror the posting's language,
remove anything outdated, and tell me which required skill is missing
so I can decide whether to learn it or leave it off.

Prompt 11 — Trim to one page without losing evidence.

My resume is too long. Cut it to one page while keeping the strongest
proof: keep bullets with numbers and recent roles, merge older roles
into one line each, and remove objective statements. Do not remove
dates. Show me what you cut so I can approve it.

Prompt 12 — Compare your resume to the job description.

Compare my resume [PASTE] with this job description [PASTE JD].
Score my match on a 1–10 scale for skills, experience level, and
keywords. Then list the three biggest gaps and the single most
important change I should make before applying.
ATS reality check: keyword matching helps you get past software, but it does not get you hired. Never let ChatGPT stuff keywords you cannot back up, and keep the layout simple: one column, standard headings, no tables or graphics for the resume you upload, because exotic formatting is what actually breaks parsers.

ChatGPT Cover Letter Prompts That Match the Job

A cover letter that repeats your resume wastes everyone's time. The best cover letters use the Hook-Story-Ask pattern: one specific reason you are excited, one story that proves you can do the job, and one clear ask. These ChatGPT cover letter prompts generate drafts you can personalize in minutes.

Prompt 13 — Draft a letter from the job description.

Write a cover letter for [JOB TITLE] at [COMPANY] using the
Hook-Story-Ask structure. Hook: one specific thing about the company
or role (from the job description below). Story: the achievement from
my fact bank that best proves I can do this job. Ask: a confident
closing line requesting an interview. Keep it under 250 words.
Job description: [PASTE JD]. Fact bank: [FACTS].

Prompt 14 — Generate three opening lines.

Write three different first sentences for my cover letter to
[COMPANY]: one that names a result I achieved, one that connects to a
detail in their job description, and one that shows I researched the
company. Then recommend which to use and why.

Prompt 15 — Cover letter for a career change.

I am applying from [OLD FIELD] to [NEW FIELD]. Write a cover letter
that addresses the switch directly in the first paragraph, translates
two achievements from my fact bank into the new context, and shows I
understand the new role's core demands: [DEMANDS FROM JD].

Prompt 16 — A short note when there is no posting.

Draft a 120-word email to [HIRING MANAGER NAME, or blank] expressing
interest in [ROLE/FIELD] at [COMPANY]. Mention one recent company
milestone I found (I will paste it below) and one relevant result of
mine. End by asking for a 15-minute chat. Keep the tone warm and
specific, never salesy. Company detail: [PASTE]. My result: [FACT].

LinkedIn, Networking, Interview, and Follow-Up Prompts

Your resume gets you screened; the rest of the funnel gets you hired. These prompts cover LinkedIn presence, outreach messages, interview rehearsal, and the follow-ups that recruiters notice because almost nobody sends them.

LinkedIn and networking prompts

Prompt 17 — Write a LinkedIn headline that is not your job title.

My current role is [TITLE] and my target role is [TARGET]. Write 5
LinkedIn headlines under 220 characters that combine what I do, who I
help, and a proof point. Avoid generic words like "guru", "ninja",
or "passionate". Here is a fact I am proud of: [FACT].

Prompt 18 — Turn your resume summary into an About section.

Turn my resume summary into a first-person LinkedIn About section
that tells a mini-story: what I do, a result I am proud of, and what
I am looking for next. Two versions: one for recruiters, one for
peers. Resume summary: [PASTE].

Prompt 19 — Request an informational interview.

Write a LinkedIn message to [PERSON] at [COMPANY] asking for a
15-minute informational chat about [ROLE/TEAM]. Reference one genuine
detail about their work or the company (I will paste it), show I have
done homework on the role, and make it easy to say yes. Under 100
words. Company detail: [PASTE].

Prompt 20 — Ask for a referral without being awkward.

Draft a message asking [CONTACT] for a referral to [ROLE] at
[COMPANY]. I worked with them at [CONTEXT]. Include: a reminder of
our connection, why I fit the role in one line, and a respectful way
to decline. Under 120 words.

Interview prep prompts

Prompt 21 — Rehearse a mock interview for the exact role.

Act as a senior interviewer at [COMPANY] hiring for [JOB TITLE].
Ask me questions one at a time based on this job description [PASTE
JD]. Start with screening questions, then move to behavioral
questions. After each answer, give brief feedback and tell me when my
answer was vague or missing a metric, then continue.

Prompt 22 — Build STAR stories from your facts.

Turn these facts into a STAR story bank: [FACTS]. For each story
give: Situation (2 lines), Task (1 line), Action (4–5 lines with
specific steps I took), Result (with the real number). Then map each
story to the common question it answers best, like leadership,
conflict, failure, or tight deadlines.

Prompt 23 — Nail "Tell me about yourself".

Write a 90-second answer to "Tell me about yourself" for [ROLE] at
[COMPANY]. Structure: present role and one proud result, a quick
bridge showing why this move makes sense, and one line on why this
company. Two versions: one confident, one warm. Facts: [PASTE].

Prompt 24 — Smart questions to ask the interviewer.

Based on this job description and company [PASTE], list 8 smart
questions I can ask the interviewer. Include: two about team dynamics,
two about how success is measured in the first 90 days, two about
challenges the team faces, and two about growth. Avoid questions
answerable from their website.

Prompt 25 — Answer the salary question.

Help me answer "What are your salary expectations?" for [ROLE] in
[CITY/REMOTE]. My market range is [RANGE from your research] and my
minimum is [MINIMUM]. Give me a one-sentence response that states a
range confidently, plus a graceful follow-up if they push for a number
before I learn more about the role.

Follow-up prompts

Prompt 26 — Post-interview thank-you note.

Write a thank-you email after my interview for [ROLE] at [COMPANY]
with [INTERVIEWER NAME]. Mention one specific topic we discussed
([TOPIC]) and reinforce one strength I showed. Keep it under 150
words, warm and specific, and end with a light forward-looking line.

Prompt 27 — Polite status follow-up after silence.

It has been [N] days since my final interview for [ROLE] at
[COMPANY]. Draft a short, confident follow-up email that restates my
interest, references the timeline they mentioned ([DETAIL]), and asks
for an update. No guilt, no groveling. Under 100 words.

Prompts That Make the Output Sound Like You

The most common complaint about AI-assisted applications is that they sound like AI. Fix it the same way you fix robotic writing anywhere else: give the model a voice sample, observable rules, and an editing pass. Our full guide to making ChatGPT sound less robotic covers the method in depth; these are the resume-specific versions.

Prompt 28 — Add your voice to any resume draft.

Read this sample of my writing so you can hear my voice: [PASTE
100–200 WORDS]. Now rewrite this resume text in that voice: [PASTE].
Rules: short sentences, active verbs, plain words, no corporate
fillers like "leveraged" or "dynamic", and no buzzwords I would never
use. Keep every fact identical.

Prompt 29 — Remove AI tells from the final draft.

Scan this resume and cover letter for AI tells: "results-driven",
"passionate about", "proven track record", "think outside the box",
"synergy", "detail-oriented", and any sentence that starts with
"Responsible for". Replace each with concrete language from my fact
bank or delete it. Show every change you made.

Prompt 30 — Fact-check the whole document.

Read my full resume and cover letter. List every number, percentage,
job title, date, tool, and company name in the document. Compare each
one against my fact bank: [FACTS]. Flag anything that is not in my
fact bank or that I wrote ambiguously, so I can correct it before I
apply. Do not fix anything yourself; just report.

Prompt 31 — Final human read-through checklist.

Give me a 10-point checklist to review my resume and cover letter
before submitting, covering: facts I can defend, dates that match,
keywords from the job, typos, consistent formatting, contact info,
file name (e.g., Firstname-Lastname-Resume.pdf), and whether each
claim would survive a follow-up question in an interview.

What Not to Let AI Do

ChatGPT is a tool for a job search, not the author of your career story. Three lines you should never cross:

  • Never let it invent facts. Fabricated employers, degrees, or metrics are the one mistake that can cost you an offer after it is made. Every claim must trace to your truth bank.
  • Never send unedited output. AI drafts are starting points. Read every line, cut what sounds like you wrote it under a thesaurus, and add the specific details only you know.
  • Never copy the job description. Mirroring keywords is smart; copying sentences is obvious and often caught. Paraphrase through your own experience.

FAQ: ChatGPT Resume Prompts

What is the best ChatGPT resume prompt?

The best ChatGPT resume prompt gives the model your real facts and the target job description, then asks for three versions of each bullet that start with strong verbs and include measurable results. A vague prompt like write my resume returns generic phrasing, while a prompt built on a fact bank returns bullets you can verify and actually use.

Can ChatGPT write my resume for me?

Yes, but treat ChatGPT as a drafting and tailoring assistant, not an author. Feed it a fact bank of your real titles, dates, tools, and results, then ask it to structure and reword that material for each application. Every number, employer, and date in the output must come from your facts, because ChatGPT can invent plausible-sounding metrics.

How do I make a ChatGPT resume sound human, not AI?

Add voice rules to the prompt: short sentences, active verbs, concrete details, and no phrases like results-driven or proven track record. Provide a sample of your own writing for the model to imitate, then run a final pass that flags every phrase you would never say. The goal is a resume that reads like you on a good day.

Will an ATS reject a ChatGPT resume?

Applicant tracking systems do not detect AI writing; they parse text and match keywords. A ChatGPT resume is not rejected for being AI-written, but it can fail if it uses tables, graphics, or unusual formatting that breaks parsing, or if it misses the keywords in the job description. Keep the layout simple and mirror the language of the posting.

How do I tailor a ChatGPT resume to one job description?

Paste the full job description into the prompt along with your fact bank, and ask ChatGPT to identify the five most important requirements, then rewrite your summary and bullets so the same evidence emphasizes those skills. Do this per application instead of sending one generic resume, because recruiters and parsing systems both reward resumes that mirror the posting.

Do recruiters care if you use ChatGPT for your resume?

Recruiters care about accuracy, relevance, and honesty more than the tool you used. What hurts candidates is fabricated metrics, exaggerated titles, and buzzword-heavy writing that falls apart in the interview. Use ChatGPT to improve clarity and keyword matching, keep every claim true, and you are using the tool the way hiring managers expect in 2026.

Final Thoughts: ChatGPT Resume Prompts Work Only With Your Facts

Every ChatGPT resume prompt in this guide points at the same truth: the model is not the expert, you are. Your job is to hand it a fact bank and a target job description, then let it do what it is good at, which is restructuring, rewording, and tailoring your real experience until it matches what recruiters and applicant tracking systems are looking for.
